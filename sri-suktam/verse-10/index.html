The verse

॥ ऋचा १० ॥

मनसः काममाकूतिं वाचः सत्यमशीमहि।
पशूनां रूपमन्नस्य मयि श्रीः श्रयतां यशः॥

॥ श्री महालक्ष्म्यै नमः ॥

Manasaḥ kāmam ākūtiṁ vācaḥ satyam aśīmahi,
Paśūnāṁ rūpam annasya mayi śrīḥ śrayatāṁ yaśaḥ.

Meaning in short

May the sincere desires and noble intentions of my heart be fulfilled; may my words always be truthful. May I possess the abundance of cattle, the beauty of nourishment, and may prosperity and honour permanently reside in my life.

Honest wishes, truthful words, enough to eat and a good name: a grounded, modest prayer.

Key highlights

  • Kāmam Wishes: pure, noble intentions of the mind
  • Satyam Truth: honest speech is the foundation
  • Annam Nourishment: cattle and food, for self and others
  • Yaśaḥ Honour: prosperity and a good name that stay

Explore the four wishes

Tap a card to open its meaning and a question to carry with you.

KāmamWishes

Manasaḥ kāmam ākūti: the wishes and intentions of the mind. The verse accepts that we have desires but asks that they be pure and noble, aligned with good rather than ego.

Pause and reflect: Which of my wishes are born of care, and which of restlessness?

SatyamTruth

Vācaḥ satyam: truth in speech. The commentary says prosperity stands on truthfulness and integrity, and asks whether we honour our own words.

Pause and reflect: Do my words match what I do?

AnnamNourishment

Paśūnāṁ rūpam annasya: the beauty or abundance of cattle and of food. Read as nourishment, it hints at prosperity that sustains oneself and others.

Pause and reflect: Does what I have feed others as well as me?

YaśaḥHonour

Mayi śrīḥ śrayatāṁ yaśaḥ: may prosperity and honour rest in me. The prayer is for them to dwell within us, not to visit briefly.

Pause and reflect: What would help a good name and steady fortune settle in my life?

Deeper spiritual insights

Interpretation and reflection: ideas that help us read the verse, not part of the text itself.

Desire and intention (Manasaḥ kāmam ākūtiṁ)

The verse acknowledges that desires and intentions are natural, but they must be pure and noble. Reflection: are my desires aligned with good, or are they born of ego and restlessness?

Truth in speech (Vācaḥ satyam)

True prosperity stands on the foundation of truthfulness and integrity. Reflection: do I honour my words? Is my speech gentle and truthful?

Cattle and food (Paśūnāṁ rūpam annasya)

Symbolising abundance in nourishment, wealth and wellbeing. It also hints at prosperity that provides sustenance for oneself and others.

May prosperity and honour reside (Mayi śrīḥ śrayatāṁ yaśaḥ)

The prayer is for Lakshmi and honour to dwell within us, not fleetingly but permanently.

Practical meditation

  1. Visualise sitting before Goddess Lakshmi.
  2. See her gently placing a lotus in your heart, representing truthful words, noble desires and fulfilled intentions.
  3. Visualise flowing streams of milk and grains around you, symbolising nourishment and plenty.
  4. Whisper inwardly: “May my desires be pure, my speech be truthful, and may prosperity and honour take shelter in my life always.”

Reflection for today

Are my daily intentions aligned with goodness and higher purpose? Do I consistently speak truthfully and with kindness? How can I make my abundance nourishing for myself and others?

Prosperity is not just wealth. It is truth in speech, nobility in intention, fulfilment of righteous desires, good nourishment and enduring honour. This verse teaches us that prosperity is inseparable from pure desires, truthful speech, sincere effort, nourishment and honour.
