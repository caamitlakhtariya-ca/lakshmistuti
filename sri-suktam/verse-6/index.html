The verse

॥ ऋचा ६ ॥

आदित्यवर्णे तपसोऽधिजातो वनस्पतिस्तव वृक्षोऽथ बिल्वः।
तस्य फलानि तपसानुदन्तु मायान्तरायाश्च बाह्या अलक्ष्मीः॥

॥ श्री महालक्ष्म्यै नमः ॥

Āditya-varṇe tapaso ’dhijāto vanaspatis tava vṛkṣo ’tha bilvaḥ,
Tasya phalāni tapasā nudantu māyāntarāyāś ca bāhyā alakṣmīḥ.

Meaning in short

O Goddess, radiant like the sun, born of divine effort and discipline: just as the sacred bilva tree bears blessed fruits, may the results of my sincere efforts remove all obstacles. May all illusions, inner barriers and external misfortunes dissolve away.

Sun-bright and born of effort: the bilva tree is the picture of slow, sacred fruit that clears obstacles.

Key highlights

  • Āditya-varṇā Radiant: bright and life-giving like the sun
  • Tapas Effort: discipline is the root of her blessing
  • Bilva Fruitful: a sacred tree, slow-growing, divine fruit
  • Antarāya Clearing: inner confusion and outer misfortune

Explore the four images

Tap a card to open its meaning and a question to carry with you.

Āditya-varṇāRadiant

Āditya-varṇā: she has the colour and brilliance of the sun. The commentary reads prosperity as radiant, life-giving and steady, like the sun.

Pause and reflect: Am I giving light to others, or am I clouded today?

TapasEffort

Tapas is effort and discipline, and the verse says she is born of it. In the commentary, Lakshmi rewards those who strive with discipline and sincerity, not those who hope for shortcuts.

Pause and reflect: Where am I relying on shortcuts instead of steady effort?

BilvaFruitful

The bilva is a sacred tree, called here the lord of the forest and her own tree. It grows slowly, but its fruit is held to be divine.

Pause and reflect: What slow, sincere effort am I planting that will bear fruit later?

AntarāyaClearing

Māyā and antarāya are inner confusion and self-made blocks; bāhyā alakṣmī is outer misfortune. The prayer is that the fruits of our effort dissolve all three, inside and around us.

Pause and reflect: Which inner block could I name honestly today?

Notes on each word
आदित्यवर्णेĀditya-varṇe

A praise of Lakshmi’s radiant, golden, life-giving energy, like the sun that nurtures and gives light to all.

तपसोऽधिजातःTapaso ’dhijāto

True abundance comes from inner discipline, effort and purity. Nothing is random; prosperity is the fruit of sustained, righteous effort. In the traditional reading, it is the tree that is said to have sprung from the Goddess’s own austerity.

वनस्पतिस्तव वृक्षोऽथ बिल्वःVanaspatis tava vṛkṣo ’tha bilvaḥ

The bilva tree represents sacred, deeply rooted blessings, and is often associated with Lord Shiva and used in pure offerings. Symbolically, this is a blessing for deeply rooted, steady and sacred prosperity.

तस्य फलानि तपसा नुदन्तुTasya phalāni tapasā nudantu

The verse is saying: may the rewards of my effort remove every barrier between me and divine prosperity.

मायान्तरायाश्च बाह्या अलक्ष्मीःMāyāntarāyāś ca bāhyā alakṣmīḥ

A direct prayer for the removal of confusion, mental blocks, self-sabotage and outer negativity.

Deeper spiritual insights

Interpretation and reflection: ideas that help us read the verse, not part of the text itself.

Sun-like radiance (Āditya-varṇe)

Prosperity is radiant, life-giving, nourishing and unwavering, like the sun. Reflection: am I radiating positivity and life-giving energy in my actions, or am I clouded?

Prosperity rooted in tapas (effort)

Lakshmi rewards those who strive with discipline and purity. Reflection: am I relying on effort and sincerity, or hoping for shortcuts?

The bilva tree as a symbol

The bilva tree is steady, sacred and fruitful. It grows slowly, but its fruits are considered divine. Reflection: am I planting deep, sacred efforts that will bear lasting fruits?

Removing inner and outer obstacles

Often, inner confusion (Māyā) and self-imposed blocks (Antarāya) stop abundance. This verse is a powerful prayer to remove these blockages, both inside and around you.

Practical meditation

  1. Visualise a grand, sacred bilva tree, strong and unshakable, filled with golden fruits.
  2. See each fruit as the result of your effort, discipline and pure intention.
  3. Imagine those fruits falling gently to the earth, dissolving every obstacle: inner fears, doubts and outer blockages.
  4. Whisper softly inside: “May the fruits of my righteous efforts remove every block. May all illusions and negativity fall away. May I shine, steady and bright, like the sun.”

Reflection for today

What are the “bilva seeds” I am planting in my life, efforts that are sacred, slow-growing, but deeply rewarding? What inner blocks (Māyā or confusion) do I need to dissolve? Am I truly shining like the sun in my words, actions and presence, or hiding behind clouds?

This verse teaches us that prosperity is not an accident. It is the divine fruit of effort, discipline and clarity. Obstacles are natural, but with steady, righteous action and inner purity, they dissolve like mist before the morning sun.
