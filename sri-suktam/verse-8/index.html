Śrī Sūktam – Verse 8

Kṣut-pipāsā-malāṁ – clearing scarcity and negativity from home and heart

By Amit N. Lakhtariya

Devanāgarī

क्षुत्पिपासामलां ज्येष्ठामलक्ष्मीं नाशयाम्यहम्।
अभूतिमसमृद्धिं च सर्वां निर्णुद मे गृहात्॥

Kṣut-pipāsā-malāṁ jyeṣṭhām alakṣmīṁ nāśayāmy aham,
Abhūtim asamṛddhiṁ ca sarvāṁ nirṇuda me gṛhāt.

Word by wordMeaningDeeper insightsMeditationReflection

Word-by-word meaning

क्षुत्पिपासामलाम्Kṣut-pipāsā-malāṁ

That which is impure due to hunger (kṣut) and thirst (pipāsā)

It also symbolises constant want, craving and dissatisfaction.

ज्येष्ठाम्Jyeṣṭhām

The elder sister (of Lakshmi)

Jyeṣṭhā is the elder sister of Lakshmi. In symbolism, she represents poverty, misfortune and quarrelsomeness: Alakṣmī’s form.

अलक्ष्मीम्Alakṣmīṁ

Misfortune

All forms of misfortune, scarcity, poverty and negativity.

नाशयाम्यहम्Nāśayāmy aham

I destroy, remove and eliminate

Through this prayer and intention.

अभूतिम्Abhūtim

Absence of prosperity; poverty

असमृद्धिं चAsamṛddhiṁ ca

And lack of growth

Stagnation and failure.

सर्वाम्Sarvāṁ

All of it, without exception

निर्णुदNirṇuda

Drive out, banish, remove forcefully

मे गृहात्Me gṛhāt

From my home

My life, my household, my heart.

Full meaning

I remove and destroy all impurity born of hunger, thirst, craving and dissatisfaction, the misfortune represented by Jyeṣṭhā and Alakṣmī. I drive out all poverty, stagnation and failure from my home and my life, completely.

Deeper spiritual insights

Kṣut-pipāsā: the root of lack

Hunger and thirst here are not just physical. They symbolise greed, dissatisfaction and endless wanting. Prosperity never stays in a place of constant craving and restlessness. If the mind is forever saying “I want this, I want that, it is not enough”, then no matter how much Lakshmi comes, she will not stay. Lakshmi loves peaceful, content spaces; a heart with a hole of dissatisfaction can never stay full. Reflection: am I living in contentment, or constantly feeling “not enough”?

Jyeṣṭhā and Alakṣmī: energies of negativity

The verse teaches that misfortune and scarcity are energies. They can take root and spread if not consciously removed. Jyeṣṭhā Devī is considered the embodiment of quarrels, scarcity, dirt and negativity, the opposite of Lakshmi. Where there is constant complaining, fighting, discontent, laziness or carelessness, that energy takes over. Reflection: are there negative patterns, complaints or toxic habits in my home or mind that I need to drive out?

Nāśayāmy aham: “I destroy”, not “I hope”

Notice the force of the words. It is a personal decision. You are not asking someone else to clean your space; you stand up and say, I will remove these energies from my mind, heart and home.

Abhūtim and Asamṛddhiṁ: poverty in every form

Not just poverty of money, but poverty of thought, love, creativity and possibility: poverty of wealth, of ideas, of relationships and of courage, and stagnation, being stuck in one place with no growth. Reflection: where am I tolerating “lack” in my thinking or surroundings?

Nirṇuda: a forceful command

This is not a meek request. It is a command to banish. It is like saying, “Enough! I will not allow scarcity and negativity to live here any more,” and sweeping out the dust and shutting the door behind it. Sometimes prosperity comes when we stop tolerating scarcity-thinking and firmly remove doubt, fear and negativity.

Practical meditation

  1. Close your eyes and visualise your home as a bright, clean space.
  2. Imagine all the dark clouds of fear, scarcity, complaints, laziness and negativity being swept out, like dust being thrown out of a sacred temple.
  3. Whisper with authority and conviction: “May all misfortune, stagnation and negativity leave my life now. My home and heart are sanctuaries of light, growth and abundance.”
  4. A simple practice: light a lamp in your home, in silence. Say aloud, “All scarcity, all doubt, all negativity, I ask you to leave. My home is now pure, blessed and filled with light.” Feel the shift in energy.

Reflection for today

What patterns of scarcity, complaining or doubt do I need to stop tolerating? What clutter, mental, emotional or physical, do I need to clean from my home and mind? How can I cultivate a home environment that attracts divine abundance? In real life: if there is clutter in the house, clear it, because Lakshmi loves cleanliness and order. If there are negative conversations, gossip or complaints every day, stop them. If your mind has fear-based thoughts about money, replace them with faith-based affirmations. If you have been procrastinating, take a small, clear action.

This verse teaches that before prosperity can truly reside, scarcity and negativity must be firmly removed. It is a cleansing, not just of home and surroundings but of mindset, attitude and heart-space. Before asking Lakshmi to enter, clean the inner temple. Remove restlessness, negativity, poverty-thinking, laziness and complaints. Then abundance flows like a river that never dries.
