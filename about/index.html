This site is a slow, verse-by-verse reading of the Śrī Sūktam and the Kanakadhāra Stotram, the well-loved hymns to Goddess Lakshmi. It began on the first day of Sharad Navratri, 2026, as a personal study and is shared here for anyone who wants to understand what they are chanting.

About the author

Amit N. Lakhtariya is a Chartered Accountant based in Ahmedabad, India, and a lifelong student of the devotional tradition. The commentary reflects his own reading and reflections on each verse.

AI assistance

This commentary is prepared collaboratively with an AI assistant (Claude, by Anthropic). The AI helps with drafting, structuring and cross-checking the word-by-word explanations. The author directs the interpretation, reviews every verse and decides what is published. Because AI can make mistakes, and because Sanskrit admits more than one reading, this is a study aid and not a substitute for a teacher or the traditional commentaries. Corrections are welcome through the contact page.

How the content is organised

Every verse page has the Devanāgarī text, a transliteration, a word-by-word meaning, the full meaning, deeper insights, a short meditation and a reflection for the day. Verses are released one at a time.

Spellings

The Goddess’s name is written Lakshmi (Sanskrit Lakṣmī), and is also commonly spelled Laxmi. Both refer to the same Goddess.
