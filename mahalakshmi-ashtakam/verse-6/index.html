Mahālakṣmī Aṣṭakam – Verse 6

Sthūla-sūkṣma – gross and subtle, fierce and all-containing

By Amit N. Lakhtariya

Devanāgarī

स्थूलसूक्ष्ममहारौद्रे महाशक्तिमहोदरे।
महापापहरे देवि महालक्ष्मि नमोऽस्तु ते॥

Sthūla-sūkṣma-mahā-raudre mahā-śakti-mahodare,
Mahā-pāpa-hare devi mahālakṣmi namo 'stu te.

Word by wordMeaningDeeper insightsMeditationReflection

Word-by-word meaning

स्थूलसूक्ष्ममहारौद्रेSthūla-sūkṣma-mahā-raudre

O you who are gross, subtle and greatly fierce

Sthūla is gross or visible; sūkṣma is subtle; raudra is fierce, terrible.

महाशक्तिMahā-śakti

Great power

Śakti is power or energy.

महोदरेMahodare

O you of great womb, who contain all

Literally “of the great belly”: she holds the whole universe within her. Some editions read mahodaye, “of great rise or prosperity”.

महापापहरेMahā-pāpa-hare

O remover of great sins

Mahā is great; pāpa is sin or fault; hara is remover.

देवि महालक्ष्मिDevi mahālakṣmi

O Goddess Mahalakshmi

Vocatives.

Full meaning

Salutations to you, O Goddess who are both gross and subtle and also greatly fierce, great power who contain all within you, remover of the greatest sins. O Mahalakshmi, I bow to you.

Deeper spiritual insights

In simple words

The verse piles up contrasts: visible and invisible, and fierce as well as nurturing. The point is that the Goddess is not one thing. She is the whole range, from the form we can see to the energy we cannot.

Gentle and fierce

Lakshmi is usually shown smiling, with coins falling from her hand. This verse reminds us that the same power can be fierce toward what harms. Taking both seriously keeps devotion from becoming sentimental.

A note on the variant reading

Editions differ on the third pāda: mahodare (of great belly, containing all) and mahodaye (of great prosperity or rise). Either way the idea is greatness and fullness. We follow the more widely printed mahodare.

Practical meditation

  1. Minute 1: still the body.
  2. Minute 2: recite the verse slowly.
  3. Minute 3: notice both the visible and the unseen in your day: what you can count, and what you cannot.
  4. Minute 4: sit with the thought that both are part of one life.

Reflection for today

What in my life is measurable, and what matters just as much but cannot be measured? Am I giving the second kind its share of attention?

Gross and subtle, gentle and fierce: the verse refuses to shrink the Goddess to one picture. That is a good habit for a reader, too. This meditation is a devotional practice for attention and reflection; it does not promise a supernatural result or guarantee material wealth.
