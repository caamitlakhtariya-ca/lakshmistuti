Mahālakṣmī Aṣṭakam – Verse 8

Śvetāmbara-dhare – in white garments, adorned with many ornaments

By Amit N. Lakhtariya

Devanāgarī

श्वेताम्बरधरे देवि नानालङ्कारभूषिते।
जगत्स्थिते जगन्मातर्महालक्ष्मि नमोऽस्तु ते॥

Śvetāmbara-dhare devi nānālaṅkāra-bhūṣite,
Jagat-sthite jagan-mātar mahālakṣmi namo 'stu te.

Word by wordMeaningDeeper insightsMeditationReflection

Word-by-word meaning

श्वेताम्बरधरेŚvetāmbara-dhare

O you who wear white garments

Śveta is white; ambara is cloth or garment; dhara is wearing.

नानालङ्कारभूषितेNānālaṅkāra-bhūṣite

O you adorned with many ornaments

Nānā is various; alaṅkāra is ornament; bhūṣita is adorned.

जगत्स्थितेJagat-sthite

O you who abide in the world

Sthita is standing, abiding, established. She is present in the world and sustains it.

जगन्मातःJagan-mātaḥ

O Mother of the world

Repeated from the seventh verse.

महालक्ष्मिMahālakṣmi

O Mahalakshmi

The refrain.

Full meaning

Salutations to you, O Goddess clad in white garments and adorned with many ornaments, who abide in the world and are its mother. O Mahalakshmi, I bow to you.

Deeper spiritual insights

In simple words

The eighth verse paints a final picture: white garments, rich ornaments, the world as her place and the whole world as her child. It closes the hymn with an image of simplicity and abundance together.

White and ornaments

White suggests purity and simplicity; ornaments suggest richness. The verse gives both without any sense of conflict. That is a gentle idea of prosperity: abundance that stays clean.

A note on colour

This hymn describes the Goddess in white. Popular images of Lakshmi, especially at Diwali, often show her in red or pink. The tradition has many forms of the Goddess, and different texts and temples describe different ones.

Practical meditation

  1. Minute 1: breathe and settle.
  2. Minute 2: recite the whole eight-verse hymn in your mind, one image per verse.
  3. Minute 3: choose the image that stayed with you most.
  4. Minute 4: carry it into the next hour.

Reflection for today

Which image in this hymn spoke to me most: the bow, the fear removed, the all-knowing mother, the lotus, the white garment? What does that say about what I am looking for?

The eight verses end as they began, with a bow. In between they have shown the Goddess as protector, giver, primal power, supreme mother. This meditation is a devotional practice for attention and reflection; it does not promise a supernatural result or guarantee material wealth.
