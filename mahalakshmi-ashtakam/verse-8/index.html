The verse

श्वेताम्बरधरे देवि नानालङ्कारभूषिते।
जगत्स्थिते जगन्मातर्महालक्ष्मि नमोऽस्तु ते॥

Śvetāmbara-dhare devi nānālaṅkāra-bhūṣite,
Jagat-sthite jagan-mātar mahālakṣmi namo 'stu te.

Meaning in short

Salutations to you, O Goddess clad in white garments and adorned with many ornaments, who abide in the world and are its mother. O Mahalakshmi, I bow to you.

White garments and many ornaments: the Goddess is pictured as simple and splendid at once, present in the world as its mother.

Key highlights

  • Śvetāmbara White: clad in white garments
  • Alaṅkāra Ornaments: adorned with many ornaments
  • Jagat-sthitā In the world: she abides in the world
  • Jagan-mātā Mother: the mother of the world

Explore the four images

Tap a card to open its meaning and a question to carry with you.

ŚvetāmbaraWhite

White is the colour of purity and calm in many Indian traditions. This verse describes the Goddess in white. The hymn gives simplicity a place in her picture.

Pause and reflect: Where could I simplify and feel lighter?

AlaṅkāraOrnaments

Nānā-alaṅkāra means many ornaments. Beauty and richness are not hidden in this picture. The hymn sets white simplicity and many ornaments side by side without choosing between them.

Pause and reflect: Can I enjoy good things without being owned by them?

Jagat-sthitāIn the world

Jagat-sthite means one who abides in the world. She is not far away. The verse places her inside the world itself, not only in a temple or a story.

Pause and reflect: Where in an ordinary day can I notice something worth honouring?

Jagan-mātāMother

Jagan-mātar, mother of the world, also closes the previous verse. Here the whole picture, dress and ornaments, is gathered under that one tender name.

Pause and reflect: How can I treat the people in my care as a mother would?

Deeper spiritual insights

Interpretation and reflection: ideas that help us read the verse, not part of the text itself.

In simple words

The eighth verse paints a final picture: white garments, rich ornaments, the world as her place and the whole world as her child. It closes the hymn with an image of simplicity and abundance together.

White and ornaments

White suggests purity and simplicity; ornaments suggest richness. The verse gives both without any sense of conflict. That is a gentle idea of prosperity: abundance that stays clean.

A note

A note on colour

This hymn describes the Goddess in white. Popular images of Lakshmi, especially at Diwali, often show her in red or pink. The tradition has many forms of the Goddess, and different texts and temples describe different ones.
