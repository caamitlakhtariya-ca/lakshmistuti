The verse

पद्मासनस्थिते देवि परब्रह्मस्वरूपिणि।
परमेशि जगन्मातर्महालक्ष्मि नमोऽस्तु ते॥

Padmāsana-sthite devi para-brahma-svarūpiṇi,
Parameśi jagan-mātar mahālakṣmi namo 'stu te.

Meaning in short

Salutations to you, O Goddess seated on the lotus, whose very nature is the Supreme Brahman, supreme sovereign and mother of the world. O Mahalakshmi, I bow to you.

Seated on the lotus, supreme in nature and mother of the world: the verse joins the highest idea with the closest one.

Key highlights

  • Padmāsana Lotus seat: she sits on a lotus
  • Para-brahma Supreme: her nature is the Supreme Brahman
  • Parameśī Sovereign: the supreme ruler
  • Jagan-mātā Mother: the mother of the world

Explore the four images

Tap a card to open its meaning and a question to carry with you.

PadmāsanaLotus seat

The lotus grows from mud and stays unstained by water. The tradition uses it as a seat of purity and calm. She sits where beauty rises from ordinary soil.

Pause and reflect: Where in my own life has something good grown from difficult ground?

Para-brahmaSupreme

Para-brahma is the Supreme Brahman, the ultimate reality. The verse says her very nature is this. The same Lakshmi shown with coins and flowers is, in this line, praised as the highest truth.

Pause and reflect: Do I look for the highest in ordinary things, or only far away?

ParameśīSovereign

Parameśī is the supreme sovereign, and it follows the line on Brahman. The title is about being at the head of all, not about displaying power.

Pause and reflect: What would it mean to lead from calm instead of pressure?

Jagan-mātāMother

The verse ends with the closest image of all: the mother of the world. After the highest idea, the hymn speaks in the most familiar voice, that of a child to a mother.

Pause and reflect: Whose care for me have I never properly thanked?

Deeper spiritual insights

Interpretation and reflection: ideas that help us read the verse, not part of the text itself.

In simple words

Two images come together: the Goddess on her lotus seat, and the Goddess as the Supreme Reality itself. Lakshmi is not only a figure in a story; here she is named as the ultimate.

The lotus seat

The lotus grows in mud yet stays clean, which is why it has long stood for purity and for being at ease in the world. To place the Goddess on a lotus is to say that prosperity and purity belong together.

Mother of the world

Jagan-mātā is a warm title. It means that the Supreme is also a mother: someone from whom we are not separate and to whom we can speak directly. Many devotees find this the most approachable image in the hymn.

Practical meditation

  1. Minute 1Sit and settle.
  2. Minute 2Picture a lotus resting on still water.
  3. Minute 3Recite the verse and let the word “mother” soften the recitation.
  4. Minute 4Rest quietly.

Reflection for today

What would it mean to approach prosperity the way a lotus approaches water: at home in it, but not stained by it?

The seventh verse joins the highest philosophy with the warmest title: Supreme Brahman, and mother. Both are meant to be held together.

This meditation is a devotional practice for attention and reflection; it does not promise a supernatural result or guarantee material wealth.
