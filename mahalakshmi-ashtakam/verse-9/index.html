Mahālakṣmī Aṣṭakam – Phalaśruti (Verses 9–11)

Phalaśruti – the fruit of reciting the hymn

By Amit N. Lakhtariya

Devanāgarī

महालक्ष्म्यष्टकं स्तोत्रं यः पठेद्भक्तिमान्नरः।
सर्वसिद्धिमवाप्नोति राज्यं प्राप्नोति सर्वदा॥
एककाले पठेन्नित्यं महापापविनाशनम्।
द्विकालं यः पठेन्नित्यं धनधान्यसमन्वितः॥
त्रिकालं यः पठेन्नित्यं महाशत्रुविनाशनम्।
महालक्ष्मीर्भवेन्नित्यं प्रसन्ना वरदा शुभा॥

Mahālakṣmy-aṣṭakaṁ stotraṁ yaḥ paṭhed bhaktimān naraḥ,
Sarva-siddhim avāpnoti rājyaṁ prāpnoti sarvadā.
Eka-kāle paṭhen nityaṁ mahā-pāpa-vināśanam,
Dvi-kālaṁ yaḥ paṭhen nityaṁ dhana-dhānya-samanvitaḥ.
Tri-kālaṁ yaḥ paṭhen nityaṁ mahā-śatru-vināśanam,
Mahālakṣmīr bhaven nityaṁ prasannā varadā śubhā.

Word-by-word meaning

महालक्ष्म्यष्टकं स्तोत्रम्Mahālakṣmy-aṣṭakaṁ stotram

The eight-verse hymn to Mahalakshmi

Aṣṭaka is a set of eight; stotra is a hymn of praise.

यः पठेत् भक्तिमान् नरःYaḥ paṭhet bhaktimān naraḥ

The person who recites it with devotion

Bhaktimān is one who has devotion.

सर्वसिद्धिम् अवाप्नोतिSarva-siddhim avāpnoti

Attains all accomplishments

Siddhi is accomplishment; avāpnoti is attains.

राज्यं प्राप्नोतिRājyaṁ prāpnoti

Obtains sovereignty or a kingdom

Classical language for authority and prosperity; many readers take it figuratively.

एककालेEka-kāle

Once a day

Kāla is time or period.

द्विकालम् / त्रिकालम्Dvi-kālam / Tri-kālam

Twice a day / three times a day

Morning and evening, or morning, noon and evening.

धनधान्यसमन्वितःDhana-dhānya-samanvitaḥ

Endowed with wealth and grain

Dhana is wealth; dhānya is grain; samanvita is endowed with.

प्रसन्ना वरदा शुभाPrasannā varadā śubhā

Pleased, boon-giving and auspicious

Three words describing Mahalakshmi as she becomes favourable.

Full meaning

Whoever recites this eight-verse hymn to Mahalakshmi with devotion is said to attain every accomplishment and lasting authority. Recited once daily, it destroys great sins; twice daily, it brings wealth and grain; three times daily, it destroys great enemies, and Mahalakshmi becomes ever pleased, boon-giving and auspicious.

Deeper spiritual insights

In simple words

Like many Sanskrit hymns, this one ends with a phalaśruti, a statement of the fruit that tradition associates with reciting it. Three more verses are added to the eight, so the hymn is often printed with eleven verses. Here the three are taken together as the ninth entry.

Once, twice, three times

The verses raise the benefit as the practice grows: once a day for forgiveness of faults, twice for prosperity, three times for strength against what opposes us. The pattern encourages regularity rather than a single grand effort.

How to read these promises

A phalaśruti states what tradition says, not what anyone can guarantee. Readers differ: some take the fruits literally, others as inner growth such as steadiness, clarity and courage. This site reads it as encouragement to practise, and does not promise an outcome.

Practical meditation

  1. Minute 1: sit and breathe.
  2. Minute 2: recite one verse of the hymn that you remember.
  3. Minute 3: choose a realistic rhythm for yourself: once a day, or once a week.
  4. Minute 4: end with a bow.

Reflection for today

What would a gentle, sustainable practice look like for me: a few minutes once a day, rather than a promise I cannot keep?

The hymn closes by turning from praise to practice. Whatever fruit one expects, the steady habit of attention is the part within our own hands. This meditation is a devotional practice for attention and reflection; it does not promise a supernatural result or guarantee material wealth.
