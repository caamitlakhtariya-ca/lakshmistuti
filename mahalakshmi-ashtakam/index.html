Mahālakṣmī Aṣṭakam

The Mahalakshmi Ashtakam (also written Mahalaxmi Ashtak or Mahalakshmyashtakam) is an eight-verse hymn to Goddess Mahalakshmi, traditionally ascribed to Indra. Chant the full text below, then read the meaning of each verse.

देवनागरी

इन्द्र उवाच

नमस्तेऽस्तु महामाये श्रीपीठे सुरपूजिते।
शङ्खचक्रगदाहस्ते महालक्ष्मि नमोऽस्तु ते॥
॥ १ ॥

नमस्ते गरुडारूढे कोलासुरभयङ्करि।
सर्वपापहरे देवि महालक्ष्मि नमोऽस्तु ते॥
॥ २ ॥

सर्वज्ञे सर्ववरदे सर्वदुष्टभयङ्करि।
सर्वदुःखहरे देवि महालक्ष्मि नमोऽस्तु ते॥
॥ ३ ॥

सिद्धिबुद्धिप्रदे देवि भुक्तिमुक्तिप्रदायिनि।
मन्त्रमूर्ते सदा देवि महालक्ष्मि नमोऽस्तु ते॥
॥ ४ ॥

आद्यन्तरहिते देवि आद्यशक्तिमहेश्वरि।
योगजे योगसम्भूते महालक्ष्मि नमोऽस्तु ते॥
॥ ५ ॥

स्थूलसूक्ष्ममहारौद्रे महाशक्तिमहोदरे।
महापापहरे देवि महालक्ष्मि नमोऽस्तु ते॥
॥ ६ ॥

पद्मासनस्थिते देवि परब्रह्मस्वरूपिणि।
परमेशि जगन्मातर्महालक्ष्मि नमोऽस्तु ते॥
॥ ७ ॥

श्वेताम्बरधरे देवि नानालङ्कारभूषिते।
जगत्स्थिते जगन्मातर्महालक्ष्मि नमोऽस्तु ते॥
॥ ८ ॥

महालक्ष्म्यष्टकं स्तोत्रं यः पठेद्भक्तिमान्नरः।
सर्वसिद्धिमवाप्नोति राज्यं प्राप्नोति सर्वदा॥
॥ ९ ॥

एककाले पठेन्नित्यं महापापविनाशनम्।
द्विकालं यः पठेन्नित्यं धनधान्यसमन्वितः॥
॥ १० ॥

त्रिकालं यः पठेन्नित्यं महाशत्रुविनाशनम्।
महालक्ष्मीर्भवेन्नित्यं प्रसन्ना वरदा शुभा॥
॥ ११ ॥

Transliteration (IAST)

Indra uvāca

Namas te 'stu mahāmāye śrīpīṭhe sura-pūjite,
Śaṅkha-cakra-gadā-haste mahālakṣmi namo 'stu te.
(1)

Namas te garuḍārūḍhe kolāsura-bhayaṅkari,
Sarva-pāpa-hare devi mahālakṣmi namo 'stu te.
(2)

Sarvajñe sarva-varade sarva-duṣṭa-bhayaṅkari,
Sarva-duḥkha-hare devi mahālakṣmi namo 'stu te.
(3)

Siddhi-buddhi-prade devi bhukti-mukti-pradāyini,
Mantra-mūrte sadā devi mahālakṣmi namo 'stu te.
(4)

Ādyanta-rahite devi ādya-śakti-maheśvari,
Yogaje yoga-sambhūte mahālakṣmi namo 'stu te.
(5)

Sthūla-sūkṣma-mahā-raudre mahā-śakti-mahodare,
Mahā-pāpa-hare devi mahālakṣmi namo 'stu te.
(6)

Padmāsana-sthite devi para-brahma-svarūpiṇi,
Parameśi jagan-mātar mahālakṣmi namo 'stu te.
(7)

Śvetāmbara-dhare devi nānālaṅkāra-bhūṣite,
Jagat-sthite jagan-mātar mahālakṣmi namo 'stu te.
(8)

Mahālakṣmy-aṣṭakaṁ stotraṁ yaḥ paṭhed bhaktimān naraḥ,
Sarva-siddhim avāpnoti rājyaṁ prāpnoti sarvadā.
(9)

Eka-kāle paṭhen nityaṁ mahā-pāpa-vināśanam,
Dvi-kālaṁ yaḥ paṭhen nityaṁ dhana-dhānya-samanvitaḥ.
(10)

Tri-kālaṁ yaḥ paṭhen nityaṁ mahā-śatru-vināśanam,
Mahālakṣmīr bhaven nityaṁ prasannā varadā śubhā.
(11)

Verses 9–11 are the phalaśruti, the closing verses on the fruit of reciting the hymn. Many people chant only the first eight. Editions differ in the wording of a few words; where they do, the verse page says so.

Meaning, verse by verse

Verse 1

Namas te ’stu Mahāmāye – the first bow

Verse 2

Garuḍārūḍhe – she who rides Garuḍa and removes fear and fault

Verse 3

Sarvajñe – the all-knowing, boon-giving mother

Verse 4

Siddhi, buddhi, bhukti, mukti – success and wisdom, enjoyment and freedom

Verse 5

Ādyanta-rahite – without beginning and without end

Verse 6

Sthūla-sūkṣma – gross and subtle, fierce and all-containing

Verse 7

Padmāsana-sthite – seated on the lotus, mother of the world

Verse 8

Śvetāmbara-dhare – in white garments, adorned with many ornaments

Phalaśruti (9–11)

Phalaśruti – the fruit of reciting the hymn

About the hymn

An aṣṭaka is a hymn of eight verses. In the Mahālakṣmī Aṣṭakam each verse ends with the same line, Mahālakṣmi namo ’stu te, “O Mahalakshmi, I bow to you”, so the refrain gives the hymn a steady rhythm for chanting. Tradition ascribes it to Indra, king of the gods, and places it in the Padma Purāṇa; accounts of the occasion differ.

The verses move from the Goddess seated at her sacred seat and worshipped by the gods, to her protection and her gifts, to her nature as the primal power and the mother of the world. The closing three verses describe the fruit of reciting the hymn, as tradition tells it. This commentary reads those as encouragement to practise, and does not promise any outcome.

There is no single rule for how to chant it. Many people recite after a bath, before a lamp and an image of the Goddess, and Fridays and Diwali are popular times. Follow your own family tradition or teacher where they differ from what is written here.
