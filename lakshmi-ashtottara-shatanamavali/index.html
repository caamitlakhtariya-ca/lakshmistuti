The 108 Names of Goddess Lakshmi

The 108 names (aṣṭottara-śatanāmāvalī) are chanted one by one, each preceded by Oṁ and followed by namaḥ, as a salutation. Here they are in Devanāgarī and transliteration, with a short meaning for each.

How the names are chanted

Each name is recited in the form Oṁ + name + namaḥ, for example ॐ प्रकृत्यै नमः (Oṁ prakṛtyai namaḥ). The name is in the dative case (“to …”), so the line means “Salutations to the one who is …”. The meaning shown under each name is the name itself. A common way to use the list is to offer a flower or a few grains of rice at each name, or simply to recite it with attention on a Friday. See the Friday chanting guide.

A note on the text

This is the commonly printed Lakshmi aṣṭottara-śatanāmāvalī of 108 names. Editions differ in a few names and in the order of some, so where your family or teacher uses a different reading, follow that. The name Devī appears twice (numbers 50 and 102), as in the usual text. The meanings are short glosses, not a full commentary; the Devanāgarī is also available as a plain PDF. Other texts to chant: Śrī Sūktam, Kanakadhāra Stotram and Mahālakṣmī Aṣṭakam.
