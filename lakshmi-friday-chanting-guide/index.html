A simple guide for devotees

Chanting for Lakshmi on Fridays

Friday is the day many families keep for Goddess Lakshmi. This is a short, flexible guide to setting up, what to chant, and how to chant with understanding.

Home › Friday chanting guide

This guide is general and non-prescriptive. Families and teachers follow different customs, and where you have a tradition or a teacher, follow that first.

Why Friday?

In many Hindu households Friday is the day kept for Goddess Lakshmi, and Fridays are a common day for her worship and for chanting her hymns. There is no need to wait for Friday, though. You can chant on any day, and many people also chant on Diwali, on the new moon and on festival days of Lakshmi.

A simple setting

  • Bathe or wash, and wear clean clothes if you can.
  • Choose a quiet, tidy corner. A picture or idol of the Goddess, or a Śrī Yantra, helps the mind settle.
  • Light a lamp (dīpa). Flowers and a simple offering of sweets or fruit are traditional.
  • Sit comfortably. Face the picture, or face east if that is your custom.

What to chant

The Śrī Sūktam

A Vedic hymn to Śrī (Lakshmi). Longer, and best with a teacher or a recording to guide the intonation.

A devotional hymn attributed to Śaṅkarācārya, with the story of the gooseberry behind it.

The Mahālakṣmī Aṣṭakam

Eight short verses, a good starting point for a beginner.

You do not have to chant all of them. One hymn, chanted with attention, is a complete practice. Many people chant one hymn on a given Friday and move to another the next week.

A 20-minute routine

  1. Settle (2 minutes). Sit, light the lamp, and take a few quiet breaths.
  2. Read the meaning first (3 minutes). Read the meaning of the verse or the hymn you will chant, so that the words carry their sense.
  3. Chant (10 minutes). Chant slowly from the Devanāgarī PDF. If you are a beginner, read the transliteration on each verse page alongside it.
  4. Close (5 minutes). Sing the Lakshmi Aarti, offer the prasād, and sit quietly for a minute.

Tips for pronunciation

  • Go slowly. A slow, clear recitation is better than a fast, blurred one.
  • Long vowels (ā, ī, ū) are held twice as long as short ones.
  • If you can, learn the Śrī Sūktam from a teacher or a trusted recording. The Vedic intonation is best learned by ear.
  • Do not worry about perfection. Devotion and attention matter most.

A word on expectations

The hymns pray for prosperity in the widest sense: a settled mind, a good household, enough for oneself and to share. They are not a guarantee of any result, and chanting is no substitute for sound judgement and honest work. The Kanakadhāra story itself says that generosity comes first.
