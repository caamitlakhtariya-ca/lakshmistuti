Kanakadhāra Stotram

A hymn to Goddess Lakshmi, traditionally attributed to Śrī Śaṅkarācārya, whose name means “the stream of gold”.

By Amit N. Lakhtariya

What the name means

Kanaka means gold and dhārā means a stream or a steady flow. The Kanakadhāra Stotram is therefore the “hymn of the golden stream”: a prayer in which Lakshmi’s grace is pictured as something that flows, patiently and without break, into a life that is ready to receive it.

The story behind it

Tradition tells of the young Śaṅkara, still a boy and a student of the sacred texts, going out to beg alms for his meal. He stopped at the door of a very poor Brahmin woman. She had nothing to give, but she searched her house and found a single dried gooseberry (āmalaka), which she offered him with great humility.

Moved by the sincerity of a gift that cost her everything she had, Śaṅkara sang a hymn to Lakshmi on the spot. In reply, the story says, Lakshmi showered the woman’s house with gooseberries made of gold. The hymn is remembered as the song that was sung that day.

Whatever one makes of the details, the point of the story is clear and is worth keeping in mind while reading the hymn: it is the giving, small as it was, that opened the door, and not the amount.

How many verses?

You will see different counts. Many printed and online versions carry 21 or 22 stanzas, with the last one speaking of the fruit of reciting the hymn. Some scholarly editions of Śaṅkarācārya’s works give a shorter core text of 18, treating the rest as later additions. This commentary will follow one clear edition, and will say so openly, so that you can follow along with whichever text you chant from.

What to expect here

The same way of reading as for the Śrī Sūktam: each verse with its Devanāgarī and transliteration, a word-by-word meaning, the full meaning in plain English, deeper insights, a short meditation and a reflection for the day. Verses will be published one at a time. The Śrī Sūktam commentary is complete and is a good place to begin while this one is prepared.
