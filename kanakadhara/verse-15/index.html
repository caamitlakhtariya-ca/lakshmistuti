The verse

॥ श्लोक १५ ॥

नमोऽस्तु कान्त्यै कमलेक्षणायै
नमोऽस्तु भूत्यै भुवनप्रसूत्यै।
नमोऽस्तु देवादिभिरर्चितायै
नमोऽस्तु नन्दात्मजवल्लभायै॥

॥ श्री महालक्ष्म्यै नमः ॥

Namo ’stu kāntyai kamalekṣaṇāyai
Namo ’stu bhūtyai bhuvana-prasūtyai;
Namo ’stu devādibhir arcitāyai
Namo ’stu nandātmaja-vallabhāyai.

Meaning in short

Let there be a bow to her who is loveliness and whose eyes are lotuses. Let there be a bow to her who is prosperity, the mother of the worlds. Let there be a bow to her who is worshipped by the gods and others. Let there be a bow to her who is the beloved of Nanda’s son.

Four bows to her radiance, her prosperity, the worship she receives and the one she loves.

Key highlights

  • Kānti Radiance: loveliness with lotus eyes
  • Bhūti Prosperity: well-being, mother of the worlds
  • Arcitā Worship: honoured by the gods and others
  • Nandātmaja Beloved: of the son of Nanda

Explore the four bows

Tap a card to open its meaning and a question to carry with you.

KāntiRadiance

Kānti is loveliness and radiance, the glow that draws the eye. The first line bows to her as radiance and as lotus-eyed. A reflection: a first sign of her is a certain brightness; wealth without it can feel cold.

Pause and reflect: What brings real brightness to my work and home?

BhūtiProsperity

Bhūti comes from bhū, “to be”: being, flourishing, prosperity. The second line bows to her as prosperity and as mother of the worlds. A reflection: here prosperity is a way of being fully alive, not only an amount.

Pause and reflect: Where am I only surviving, and where am I thriving?

ArcitāWorship

Arcitā means worshipped, and devādi means the gods and others. In Verse 13 her mercy went out to the gods; here their worship comes to her. A reflection: the verse puts honour and mercy in both directions.

Pause and reflect: What do I honour in practice, not only in word?

NandātmajaBeloved

Nandātmaja is the son of Nanda, that is Krishna. It is the fifth name in a row for her beloved: Puruṣottama, Nārāyaṇa, Śārṅgāyudha, Dāmodara and now Nandātmaja, while she stays the same.

Pause and reflect: What stays the same in me while my circumstances change?

Notes on each word
नमः अस्तुNamo ’stu

The familiar opening: namaḥ, a bow, and astu, “let it be”, joined as namo ’stu. It begins each of the four lines.

कान्त्यैKāntyai

Kānti is loveliness, radiance, a beauty that attracts, from a root meaning “to desire, to shine”. It is a feminine word ending in -i, so its “to” form is -yai: kāntyai. It is one of the Goddess’s names for her shining beauty.

कमलेक्षणायैKamala-īkṣaṇāyai

Kamala is a lotus; īkṣaṇa is the eye, from the root īkṣ, “to see”. So she is lotus-eyed, the same picture as saroruhākṣi in a later verse. The a and ī join into e, giving kamalekṣaṇā.

भूत्यैBhūtyai

Bhūti comes from the root bhū, “to be, to become”. It means being, flourishing, prosperity, well-being. It is a feminine word in -i, so its “to” form is bhūtyai. In the tradition it is a name of Lakshmi herself.

भुवनप्रसूत्यैBhuvana-prasūtyai

Bhuvana is a world; prasūti is bringing forth, birth, and so a mother or a source. So she is the one from whom the worlds are brought forth. The ending -yai is again “to”, from prasūti.

देवादिभिरर्चितायैDevādibhir arcitāyai

Devādi is “the gods and the rest”; -bhiḥ means “by”, in the plural. Arcitā is “worshipped, honoured”, from the root arc. The visarga of -bhiḥ becomes r before the vowel a, so it is written devādibhir arcitāyai.

नन्दात्मजवल्लभायैNanda-ātmaja-vallabhāyai

Nanda is the cowherd chief who raised Krishna; ātmaja is “born of oneself”, a son. So nandātmaja is the son of Nanda, that is Krishna, a form of Vishnu. Vallabhā is a beloved. She is the beloved of Nanda’s son.

Sanskrit structure

How the verse is built. Grammar only, with no interpretation.

Anvaya The sentence in plain order

Each line is namo ’stu followed by descriptions in the “to” form (the dative). The word tasyai, “to her”, is understood. Lines one and two each carry two descriptions: the name, then a description of her. The lines already stand in prose order.

The patternThe description in the “to” form
namo ’stukāntyai kamalekṣaṇāyai
namo ’stubhūtyai bhuvana-prasūtyai
namo ’studevādibhir arcitāyai
namo ’stunandātmaja-vallabhāyai

Vibhakti Why the endings are -yai, -āyai and -bhiḥ

Namaḥ, “a bow”, takes the “to” form, the dative (Pāṇini 2.3.16). A feminine word in -i, such as kānti, bhūti or prasūti, takes -yai: kāntyai, bhūtyai, prasūtyai. A feminine word in -ā takes -āyai: īkṣaṇāyai, arcitāyai, vallabhāyai.

In the third line, devādibhiḥ is the “by” form (instrumental) in the plural: “by the gods and others”. It goes with arcitāyai, “to her who is worshipped”, so the line reads “to her who is worshipped by the gods and others”. The visarga of -bhiḥ becomes r before the vowel of arcitāyai.

Deeper spiritual insights

Interpretation and reflection: ideas that help us read the verse, not part of the text itself.

In simple words

“O Mother, I bow to you, radiant and lotus-eyed. I bow to you, prosperity itself and mother of the worlds. I bow to you, whom the gods and others worship. I bow to you, beloved of Nanda’s son.” Four bows, one in each line.

From the text

What the verse actually says

This is the third verse in a row built of four bows. The descriptions are: Kānti, lotus-eyed; Bhūti, the mother of the worlds; she who is worshipped by the gods and others; and the beloved of Nanda’s son. Two of them are names of the Goddess herself, Kānti and Bhūti, and the first and the second line each carry a second description.

Picture it

The poetry hidden in the verse

Imagine four doors into the same room. One opens on her face: shining, with eyes like lotuses. One opens on what she is: well-being itself, from which the worlds come. One opens on those who honour her: the gods and others. One opens on the one she loves. Whichever door you take, she is in the room.

Kānti, radiance

Kānti is the glow of beauty, the quality that draws the eye. The poet bows to her as radiance itself and, in the same line, as lotus-eyed. As a reflection: a first sign of her is a certain brightness; wealth that is not accompanied by it can feel cold.

Bhūti, prosperity and being

Bhūti shares its root with bhū, “to be”. It means prosperity, but also being and flourishing. The verse bows to prosperity as a form of the Goddess and then to the mother of the worlds, as if to say that thriving is part of how the worlds come to be. As a reflection: here prosperity is a way of being fully alive, not only an amount.

Worshipped by the gods and others

The word devādi means the gods and the rest. Even the gods are pictured as worshipping her. This is the reverse of Verse 13, where her mercy went out to the gods; here the gods’ worship comes to her. As a reflection: the verse puts honour and mercy in both directions.

Nandātmaja-vallabhā, beloved of Nanda’s son

After Puruṣottama in Verse 11, Nārāyaṇa in Verse 12, Śārṅgāyudha in Verse 13 and Dāmodara in Verse 14, here is yet another name for her beloved: the son of Nanda, the cowherd chief. Five verses, five names for one husband, always in the last line. That is a reflection on the pattern, not a line of the text.

The thread

How this verse follows the last

Verse 14 gave her birth, her place, her home and her belonging. This verse closes the run of bows with her radiance, her prosperity, her worship by the gods and her beloved. The next verse turns from the bows to what the bowing does.

Words to keep

Three Sanskrit words to keep

Kānti is radiance. Bhūti is prosperity, well-being. Prasūti is a bringing forth, a mother.

Say it aloud

How to pronounce the key words

Practical meditation

  1. Minute 1RadianceSay silently, kāntyai namaḥ. Think of someone whose face lights up a room and wish them well.
  2. Minute 2BeingSay bhūtyai namaḥ. Breathe slowly and notice that you are here, alive and able to work.
  3. Minute 3The sourceSay bhuvana-prasūtyai namaḥ. Give thanks for the world that supports your life: food, water, people.
  4. Minute 4HonourSay devādibhir arcitāyai namaḥ. Ask what you honour most, and whether your day shows it.
  5. Minute 5A bowBow your head and give thanks for what is already in your life.
