The verse

॥ श्लोक २० ॥

कमले कमलाक्षवल्लभे त्वं
करुणापूरतरङ्गितैरपाङ्गैः।
अवलोकय मामकिञ्चनानां
प्रथमं पात्रमकृत्रिमं दयायाः॥

॥ श्री महालक्ष्म्यै नमः ॥

Kamale kamalākṣa-vallabhe tvaṁ
karuṇā-pūra-taraṅgitair apāṅgaiḥ,
Avalokaya mām akiñcanānāṁ
prathamaṁ pātram akṛtrimaṁ dayāyāḥ.

Meaning in short

O Kamalā, beloved of the lotus-eyed one, look on me with your glances rippling with a flood of compassion: me, the first among those who have nothing, a genuine vessel for your mercy.

The poet asks to be looked on, and offers honest need as his only claim.

Key highlights

  • Kamale Lotus: she is called by the name of the lotus
  • Karuṇā-pūra Flood: compassion rippling in her glance
  • Akiñcana Empty: those who have nothing
  • Pātra Vessel: one who can receive her mercy

KamaleLotus

Kamalā is a name of Lakshmi, “she of the lotus”, and the poet calls her beloved of the lotus-eyed one. A reflection: the verse begins by naming who she is before it asks for anything.

Pause and reflect: How do I begin when I ask for help?

Karuṇā-pūraFlood

Karuṇā-pūra-taraṅgita is rippling with a flood of compassion. The glance is the water. A reflection: the hymn travels from describing a glance in Verse 1 to asking for one here.

Pause and reflect: Who has looked on me with real kindness?

AkiñcanaEmpty

Akiñcana is one who has nothing. Verse 8 used the word for a helpless young bird. The poet applies it to himself and goes further: prathamam, the first. A reflection: the claim on mercy is made by honesty about need.

Pause and reflect: What do I actually lack, said plainly?

PātraVessel

Pātra is a vessel and also a worthy recipient, and akṛtrima means genuine. A reflection: a full cup cannot be filled; what the verse suggests is making room.

Pause and reflect: What do I hold so tightly that nothing else fits?

Notes on each word
कमलेKamale

Kamalā is a name of Lakshmi, “she of the lotus”. The ending -e is the calling form: “O Kamalā”.

कमलाक्षवल्लभेKamalākṣa-vallabhe

Kamala is a lotus and akṣa is the eye, so kamalākṣa is “lotus-eyed”, a familiar name of Vishnu. Vallabhā is a beloved. The calling form is vallabhe.

त्वम्Tvam

The word “you”, as the one who will act: “you, look on me”.

करुणापूरतरङ्गितैःKaruṇā-pūra-taraṅgitaiḥ

Karuṇā is compassion. Pūra is a flood, a filling. Taraṅgita is “made into waves, rippling”, from taraṅga, a wave. The ending -aiḥ is “with” in the plural, and agrees with apāṅgaiḥ: glances that ripple with a flood of compassion.

अपाङ्गैःApāṅgaiḥ

Apāṅga is the outer corner of the eye, and so a sidelong glance. It is the word with which the hymn began in Verse 1. The ending -aiḥ is “with”, in the plural.

अवलोकयAvalokaya

From ava, “down”, and the root lok, “to see”. The command is to look down kindly, as one looks on someone who needs help. The same root gives the name Avalokiteśvara, “the one who looks down”.

माम्Mām

The “whom” form of “I”. The look is to fall on the poet himself.

अकिञ्चनानाम्Akiñcanānām

Kiñcana is “anything”; a-kiñcana is “not even anything”, one who has nothing of his own. The ending -ānām is “of”, in the plural: among those who have nothing. The m of mām runs into the a: mām akiñcanānām.

प्रथमम्Prathamam

Prathama means first. Here it describes the poet as the first among the destitute, the leading case.

पात्रम्Pātram

Pātra is a vessel, a cup, and also a worthy recipient of a gift. In the verse it is the one who can receive her compassion.

अकृत्रिमम्Akṛtrimam

Kṛtrima is artificial, made. A-kṛtrima is the opposite: natural, true. His need is real and he does not pretend.

दयायाःDayāyāḥ

Dayā is compassion. The ending -āyāḥ is “of”: a vessel of compassion, one fit to receive it.

Deeper spiritual insights

Interpretation and reflection: ideas that help us read the verse, not part of the text itself.

In simple words

“O lotus-born Mother, beloved of the lotus-eyed Lord, look at me with the glance that is full of compassion. Of all who have nothing, I am the first. My need is real, and I am a true vessel for your mercy.” The poet asks to be seen, and says honestly that he is empty.

From the text

What the verse actually says

The verse has one request, avalokaya, “look on me”. It says by what: glances rippling with a flood of compassion. It says on whom: the poet, the first among those who have nothing. And it says why he is fit: his need is genuine, akṛtrima, and so he is a vessel for her compassion.

Picture it

The poetry hidden in the verse

A flood that ripples into waves, and a vessel set out to receive it. The glance is the water, the poet’s empty hands the cup. Emptiness is not hidden or apologised for; it is offered as his only credential. The hymn that began with a glance returns to it, and for the first time asks for it outright.

Apāṅga returns

The word apāṅga first appeared in Verse 1, where her glance was the subject. Here it is the means: look on me with your glances. As a reflection: the hymn travels from describing a glance to asking for one.

Akiñcana, one who has nothing

Verse 8 used the same word for the helpless young bird that waits for rain. Here the poet applies it to himself, and goes further: prathamam, the first among them. As a reflection: the verse makes a claim on mercy by honesty about need, not by claims of merit.

Akṛtrima, genuine

Akṛtrima means not made, not feigned. The poet says his need is genuine and does not pretend. As a reflection: a plain account of where one actually stands is where any real asking begins.

Pātra, the vessel

A pātra is a cup that can hold, and also a worthy recipient. The two meanings meet in the verse: to be a good receiver is to be open and empty. As a reflection: a full cup cannot be filled; what the verse suggests is making room.

The thread

How this verse follows the last

Verse 19 set a vast morning scene and a bow. This verse brings the hymn back to the person: the poet, with nothing, asking to be looked on. The verses that follow close the hymn.

Words to keep

Three Sanskrit words to keep

Karuṇā is compassion. Akiñcana is one who has nothing. Pātra is a vessel, a fit recipient.

Practical meditation

  1. Minute 1The lookSay silently, avalokaya. Remember a time when someone really saw you.
  2. Minute 2The floodSay karuṇā-pūra. Picture a wave of kindness reaching the shore of your day and wish it for someone near you.
  3. Minute 3EmptinessSay akiñcana. Name honestly one thing you lack: time, patience, money, knowledge. Hold it without shame.
  4. Minute 4The vesselSay pātram. Open your hands as a cup and sit with the thought of being ready to receive.
  5. Minute 5GenuineSay akṛtrimam. Decide on one thing today in which you will be plain and honest about where you stand.

Reflection for today

  • LackWhat do I actually lack, said without dressing it up?
  • RoomWhat do I hold so tightly that nothing else can be received?
  • PlainnessWhere can I be more honest today?
