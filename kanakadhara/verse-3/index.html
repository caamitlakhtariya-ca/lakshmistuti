The verse

॥ श्लोक ३ ॥

विश्वामरेन्द्रपदविभ्रमदानदक्षम्
आनन्दहेतुरधिकं मुरविद्विषोऽपि।
ईषन्निषीदतु मयि क्षणमीक्षणार्धम्
इन्दीवरोदरसहोदरमिन्दिरायाः॥

॥ श्री महालक्ष्म्यै नमः ॥

Viśvāmarendra-pada-vibhrama-dāna-dakṣam,
Ānanda-hetur adhikaṁ mura-vidviṣo 'pi;
Īṣan niṣīdatu mayi kṣaṇam īkṣaṇārdham,
Indīvarodara-sahodaram Indirāyāḥ.

Meaning in short

May even half a glance of Indirā (Lakshmi), a glance able to bestow the splendour of Indra’s station as lord of all the gods, an abundant source of joy even to Vishnu, the foe of Mura, and as soft as the inside of a blue lotus, settle lightly upon me, if only for a moment.

A glance that could bestow Indra’s throne, and the poet asks for only half of it, for a moment.

Key highlights

  • Indra Grandeur: the splendour of Indra’s station
  • Ānanda Joy: an abundant source of joy even to Vishnu
  • Indīvara Softness: soft as the inside of a blue lotus
  • Īkṣaṇārdha Half-glance: the small request, for a moment

Explore the four images

Tap a card to open its meaning and a question to carry with you.

IndraGrandeur

The first line says her glance is skilled at bestowing the splendour of Indra’s station, the highest seat among the gods. It tells us how great the blessing is.

Pause and reflect: What would I really want from a blessing of such size?

ĀnandaJoy

Her glance is an abundant source of joy even to Vishnu, the foe of Mura. Love completes the picture: the power of the glance is also tenderness.

Pause and reflect: Do I use what power I have to bring joy to others?

IndīvaraSoftness

The blue lotus, indīvara, softens the grandeur of the first lines. The glance is as soft and deep as the inner blue of the flower. Power and gentleness are held together.

Pause and reflect: Where could I add softness to something I do with strength?

ĪkṣaṇārdhaHalf-glance

Īkṣaṇārdham is half a glance. The poet asks for neither a kingdom nor riches: only a fraction of a look, resting on him for a moment. The request is modest, the aspiration large.

Pause and reflect: What small moment of grace or insight would change my direction?

Notes on each word
विश्वामरेन्द्रपदविभ्रमदानदक्षम्Viśvāmarendra-pada-vibhrama-dāna-dakṣam

Viśva is all; amara is an immortal, a god; indra is lord, so viśvāmarendra is Indra, king of all the gods. Pada is station or rank; vibhrama is splendour, charm and the glamour of high office; dāna is giving; dakṣa is skilled or able. The final m of dakṣam joins the next word when the verse is recited without a pause (dakṣam + ānanda sounds as dakṣamānanda).

आनन्दहेतुःĀnanda-hetuḥ

Ānanda is joy, bliss; hetu is cause or source.

अधिकम्Adhikam

Read with ānanda: a source of abundant joy. Translators also render it “even more”, but the sense is the same: the joy is great.

मुरविद्विषः अपिMura-vidviṣaḥ api

Mura is the demon of that name; vidviṣ is an enemy, one opposed to. So “the enemy of Mura” is Vishnu, the same Murāri we met in Verse 2. Api is “even” or “also”.

ईषत् निषीदतुĪṣat niṣīdatu

Īṣat is slightly, a little; niṣīdatu is “may it sit down, may it rest”. The two join as īṣan niṣīdatu. The picture is of something landing gently, as a bird alights.

मयि क्षणम्Mayi kṣaṇam

Mayi is “on me”; kṣaṇa is an instant or a moment.

ईक्षणार्धम्Īkṣaṇārdham

Īkṣaṇa is a look or glance (from the root īkṣ, to see); ardha is half. This is the thing the poet asks to settle on him.

इन्दीवरोदरसहोदरम्Indīvarodara-sahodaram

Indīvara is the blue lotus; udara is the interior (it also means belly); sahodara is literally “born of the same womb”, a sibling, and so “closely resembling”. The word udara appears twice inside this one compound.

इन्दिरायाःIndirāyāḥ

Indirā is a name of Lakshmi; the ending marks “of”.

Deeper spiritual insights

Interpretation and reflection: ideas that help us read the verse, not part of the text itself.

In simple words

“O Mother Lakshmi, your glance is so powerful that it can give the glory of a heavenly throne, and it brings joy even to Lord Vishnu. I ask for so little in comparison: may a fraction of that glance rest on me for one brief moment and bless my life.” The verse praises the greatness of the glance and then makes the smallest possible request.

Picture it

The poetry hidden in the verse

Śaṅkara could have written, “Lakshmi is powerful. Please bless me.” Instead he moves our eyes from the grandeur of a heavenly throne to the delicacy of a single glance. First we picture Indra in the magnificence of his heaven. Then the poet turns to Vishnu, for whom her glance is a source of joy. Finally the grandeur becomes intimate: īṣan niṣīdatu mayi, “may it settle lightly on me”. From the throne of Indra to the humble devotee, from cosmic power to a moment of grace. A poet sees what ordinary eyes miss.

Three images, one prayer

The verse holds three pictures side by side: the grandeur of Indra, the joy of Vishnu and the soft blue lotus. Power, love and beauty are set around a single glance. The lotus is what softens the majesty of the first lines; it gives the glance something we can see, rather than merely telling us it is beautiful.

The greatness of the blessing, the humility of the request

The first line speaks of the power to grant Indra’s exalted position. Yet the poet asks for neither a kingdom nor a throne nor riches. He asks for half a glance, resting on him for a moment. The contrast suggests a devotional attitude: the devotee recognises the greatness of the Divine without treating grace as something to be bought or commanded. A useful lesson to carry: approach what you revere with humility, even when your aspirations are large.

Even the powerful look to grace

In this imagery Indra stands for the highest authority among the gods, and Vishnu for divine majesty and bliss. The verse says her glance can confer Indra’s station and is a joy to Vishnu. The poetic message is that status and power are not the whole story. In everyday life this can remind us that professional success, wealth or authority should not make us forget gratitude, relationships and the sources of our good fortune.

The meaning of “just a moment”

Kṣaṇam means “for a moment”. The poet’s request is modest in duration but large in aspiration. We often imagine that change must arrive through some grand event, yet a moment of insight, a word of wisdom or an act of compassion can turn the direction of a life. This is a reflection inspired by the verse, not a promise that a brief prayer will automatically change one’s circumstances.

The blue lotus, and a play on a word

The blue lotus (indīvara) is deeply rooted in Indian devotional and poetic imagery, where it evokes beauty, freshness and quiet colour. Here the comparison is to its inside: the glance is as soft and deep as the inner blue of the flower. There is also a small piece of sound-play. The compound indīvarodara-sahodaram carries udara twice, and it sits next to Indirāyāḥ, so the line opens and closes on a similar sound: Indīvara… Indirā. Reading the verse aloud, one hears how the lotus and the Goddess are tied together.

The thread

How this verse follows Verses 1 and 2

The first two verses showed the glance moving towards Vishnu: a sidelong glance in Verse 1 and a garland of repeated glances in Verse 2. Verse 3 spends most of its lines on how great that glance is, then makes the poet’s request in the shortest form possible, half a glance for a moment. The verses open out gradually: first we see the glance, then we learn its power, and then the devotee dares to ask.

Grace and effort

The verse asks for divine blessing. It does not prescribe a practical programme for success. We can still carry its spirit into daily life: pray with sincerity, work with integrity, manage resources prudently, and stay humble when circumstances favour us. Grace may inspire our efforts; it should not become an excuse to abandon them.

Words to keep

Three Sanskrit words to keep

Ānanda is bliss, a deep joy that goes beyond ordinary pleasure. Īkṣaṇa is a glance, the act of seeing, and here it is the very thing the devotee prays for. Dakṣa is capable or skilful; in this verse it describes the Goddess’s ability to bestow great glory.

Say it aloud

How to pronounce the key words

Viśvāmarendra: vish-vaa-ma-ren-dra. Vibhrama: vi-bhra-ma. Dakṣam: dak-sham. Mura-vidviṣaḥ: mu-ra-vid-vi-shah. Īṣan niṣīdatu: ee-shan ni-shee-da-tu. Īkṣaṇārdham: eek-sha-naar-dham. Indīvarodara: in-dee-va-ro-da-ra. Read the verse slowly and give each line time to settle; the aim is understanding, not speed.

Practical meditation

  1. Minute 1Become stillSit comfortably and breathe naturally. Let the day’s concerns settle; do not force the mind to become empty.
  2. Minutes 2–3Recite the verseRead the Sanskrit slowly, resting on īṣan niṣīdatu mayi kṣaṇam īkṣaṇārdham. Let its meaning settle: may even a fraction of the glance rest upon me for a moment.
  3. Minute 4Visualise the lotusImagine a blue lotus on still water and let it stand for the beauty and calm of the Goddess’s glance. You need not force a vision; simply hold the image.
  4. Minute 5Offer a prayer. Say silently“O Mother Lakshmi, may I receive the wisdom to recognise my blessings, the humility to remain grounded and the discernment to use my resources for good.”

Reflection for today

  • AmbitionAm I pursuing success while also building the character needed to handle it?
  • HumilityWhen things go well, do I remember the people, opportunities and circumstances that helped me?
  • GraceCan I be grateful for what I already have while continuing to work towards what I hope to achieve? I need not answer all three today; I can choose one and let it stay with me through the day.

A heavenly throne stands for extraordinary power. Vishnu stands for divine majesty and joy. The blue lotus gives the Goddess’s glance a soft, vivid beauty. Then Śaṅkara turns to his own prayer: may even half a glance rest upon me for a moment. The heart of the verse is not only how much Lakshmi can bestow; it is the humility with which her grace is sought. Aspire greatly, remain humble, and remember that prosperity is most meaningful when it comes with wisdom and gratitude.

This meditation is a devotional practice for attention and reflection; it does not promise a supernatural result or guarantee material wealth.
