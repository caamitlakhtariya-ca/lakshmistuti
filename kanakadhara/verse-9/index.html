Kanakadhāra Stotram – Verse 9

Iṣṭā viśiṣṭa-matayaḥ – the glance and the prayer for puṣṭi

By Amit N. Lakhtariya

Devanāgarī

इष्टा विशिष्टमतयोऽपि यया दयार्द्र-
दृष्ट्या त्रिविष्टपपदं सुलभं लभन्ते।
दृष्टिः प्रहृष्टकमलोदरदीप्तिरिष्टां
पुष्टिं कृषीष्ट मम पुष्करविष्टरायाः॥

Iṣṭā viśiṣṭa-matayo 'pi yayā dayārdra-
dṛṣṭyā triviṣṭapa-padaṁ sulabhaṁ labhante;
Dṛṣṭiḥ prahṛṣṭa-kamalodara-dīptir iṣṭāṁ
puṣṭiṁ kṛṣīṣṭa mama puṣkara-viṣṭarāyāḥ.

Word by wordMeaningDeeper insightsMeditationReflection

Word-by-word meaning

इष्टाःIṣṭāḥ

The cherished ones; the devoted

Iṣṭa means desired, dear, cherished; it is also used for what is honoured by worship. Here it describes the people who are meant: those dear to her, or devoted to her. Readers differ on the exact shade. In the verse it appears as iṣṭā, because the final visarga falls away before the next word.

विशिष्टमतयःViśiṣṭa-matayaḥ

Those of distinguished understanding

Viśiṣṭa is distinguished, excellent, set apart; mati is mind, understanding, intelligence. So these are people of fine understanding. Note the beginning of the word: it is viśiṣṭa, not aviśiṣṭa. Run together with the next word it becomes matayo ’pi.

अपिApi

Even; also

A small word that adds “even, too”. Here it is joined to the previous word as -yo ’pi, where the apostrophe marks a vowel that has merged.

ययाYayā

By which

A feminine “by which”, agreeing with dṛṣṭyā (glance): by which glance.

दयार्द्रदृष्ट्याDayārdra-dṛṣṭyā

By the glance moist with compassion

Dayā is compassion; ārdra is moist, tender, softened; dṛṣṭi is a look or glance. The picture is of a look so full of compassion that it is wet with it. The ending means “by”: it is by this glance that the next thing happens.

त्रिविष्टपपदम्Triviṣṭapa-padam

The station of heaven

Triviṣṭapa is heaven, the celestial world. Pada is a place, a station, a standing. The traditional meaning is the heavenly state or world.

सुलभम्Sulabham

Easily

Su- is good or well; labha is getting. So sulabha is easy to obtain, easily had. Here it is used as an adverb: they get it with ease.

लभन्तेLabhante

They obtain

From labh, to obtain. The plural subject is the devoted and the wise named at the start of the verse.

दृष्टिःDṛṣṭiḥ

The glance

The subject of the second half. From dṛś, to see. This is the same glance that the first half has described.

प्रहृष्टकमलोदरदीप्तिःPrahṛṣṭa-kamala-udara-dīptiḥ

With the radiance of the interior of a fully open lotus

Prahṛṣṭa is thrilled, delighted; for a flower, it suggests fully open, as if bristling with joy. Kamala is lotus. Udara is the inside, the middle, the body of something; for a lotus, its centre. Dīpti is brightness, shining. The glance has the glow of the heart of an open lotus. The words join as kamalodara.

इष्टाम्Iṣṭām

The desired

The same word iṣṭa as at the start of the verse, now in the singular, describing puṣṭi: the nourishment that is wished for.

पुष्टिम्Puṣṭim

Nourishment, thriving

Puṣṭi is nourishment, fullness, growth, flourishing; it is the word for becoming well-fed and well-grown, in body, family and life. See Śrī Sūktam Verse 13, where the same word appears.

कृषीष्टKṛṣīṣṭa

May it bring about

A form of kṛ, to do or make, used for a blessing or wish: “may it make, may it bring about”. It is the verb of the second half.

ममMama

For me; my

“Of me, mine”. It tells us who the prayer is for.

पुष्करविष्टरायाःPuṣkara-viṣṭarāyāḥ

Of her whose seat is a lotus

Puṣkara is a lotus; viṣṭara is a seat. So she is the one who sits on a lotus: Lakshmi. The ending means “of”: the glance belongs to her.

Full meaning

Even people who are cherished and of distinguished understanding gain the station of heaven easily by her glance, moist with compassion. May that glance of the lotus-seated Goddess, bright as the interior of a fully open lotus, bring about for me the nourishment I desire.

Deeper spiritual insights

In simple words

“O Mother, the wise and the devoted reach the highest places easily by your compassionate look. Let that same look, shining like the heart of an open lotus, give me the growth and nourishment I wish for.” After the picture of the helpless bird in Verse 8, the poet speaks of a goal he can name: puṣṭi.

What the verse actually says

The first half is a statement: those who are cherished and of excellent understanding obtain heaven easily through her glance. The second half is a wish: may her glance, radiant as an open lotus, bring about the puṣṭi I desire. Notice what is placed under her glance: even people of fine understanding. The verse does not say that the humble or the unlearned are favoured; it says that her look is what makes the way easy for everyone, including the wise.

A note on the reading

The line is iṣṭā viśiṣṭa-matayo ’pi: the wise and devoted (viśiṣṭa-matayaḥ). Some summaries read it as aviśiṣṭa, “not distinguished”, and so take the verse to be about ordinary people being lifted. Standard editions have viśiṣṭa, and so does this page. Both readings can inspire a reflection, but the verse itself is about the wise.

The sound of the verse

Listen to the verse and one sound keeps returning: ṣṭ. It appears ten times in this one verse: iṣṭā, viśiṣṭa, triviṣṭapa, dṛṣṭyā, dṛṣṭiḥ, prahṛṣṭa, iṣṭām, puṣṭim, kṛṣīṣṭa, viṣṭarāyāḥ. Śaṅkara builds the whole verse on one repeating beat. It is a poet’s pleasure, and you can hear it best when the verse is read aloud.

The glance that is wet with compassion

Dayārdra-dṛṣṭi is one of the loveliest phrases in the hymn. Ārdra means moist, as soil after rain or as eyes that have filled. The poet has just spoken of clouds in Verse 8; here the same idea returns in a smaller form. Her look is not cold or merely graceful; it is softened by feeling.

The lotus opened

The second picture is a lotus in full bloom. Prahṛṣṭa comes from a root meaning to thrill with joy, and for a flower it suggests complete opening, with nothing held back. The glance is compared to the glowing centre of that flower. A closed bud does not show its inside; an open lotus does. The comparison suggests a look that is warm and fully given.

What puṣṭi can mean

Puṣṭi is a wider word than “wealth”. It means to be nourished, to grow, to thrive. As a reflection, not a definition in the verse, we may think of strength in the body, clarity in the mind, steadiness in the family, growth in one’s work and a sense of enough. Money may be one part of this, but the word itself looks to a life that is well fed in every sense.

Knowledge and grace

The first half sets wisdom beside grace. The wise are named, and the verse still says it is by her glance that the way becomes easy. As a reflection: skill, learning and good judgement matter, and the hymn does not dismiss them. But the poet places them under something larger. Many people find this a useful reminder when their own abilities have taken them a long way and they feel tempted to think they did it all alone.

Heaven and everyday life

Triviṣṭapa is the world of the gods in tradition, and that is what the verse means. A reader may also take heaven figuratively as a life that is lifted, calmer and better. The verse does not promise that every wish will be met. It praises a compassion that makes difficult things easier.

How this verse follows the last

Verse 8 ended with a young bird waiting for rain. Verse 9 turns from need to hope. It moves from “I have nothing” to “let me be nourished”, and the cloud of the eyes becomes the lotus of the eyes. The tone is quieter and more confident.

Three Sanskrit words to keep

Dayārdra is moist with compassion. Puṣṭi is nourishment and thriving. Sulabha is easily obtained.

How to pronounce the key words

Iṣṭā: ish-taa. Viśiṣṭa-matayaḥ: vi-shish-ta-ma-ta-yah. Dayārdra-dṛṣṭyā: da-yaar-dra-drish-tyaa. Triviṣṭapa-padam: tri-vish-ta-pa-pa-dam. Sulabham: su-la-bham. Labhante: la-bhan-te. Prahṛṣṭa-kamalodara-dīptiḥ: pra-hrish-ta-ka-ma-lo-da-ra-deep-tih. Puṣṭim: push-tim. Kṛṣīṣṭa: kri-sheesh-ta. Puṣkara-viṣṭarāyāḥ: push-ka-ra-vish-ta-raa-yaah.

Practical meditation

  1. Minute 1, settle: sit comfortably, breathe slowly and imagine a lotus resting on still water at dawn.
  2. Minute 2, recite: say the phrase dayārdra-dṛṣṭi quietly, “the glance moist with compassion”. Picture a look that is kind and unhurried.
  3. Minute 3, think of a person or a responsibility in your life that needs that kind of patient attention.
  4. Minute 4, ask yourself: “Am I helping those around me to be nourished and to grow, or only trying to gain for myself?”
  5. Minute 5, offer a prayer. Say silently: “O Mother Lakshmi, may there be growth in my home, health in my body, clarity in my work and compassion in my heart. May what I gain nourish others as well.”

Reflection for today

Ability: when something has gone well for me, do I remember the help that came with my own effort? Nourishment: what would puṣṭi mean for me this year, beyond money? Compassion: whom could I look at today with a softer eye? One honest answer is enough for today.

The poet who sat as a fledgling in Verse 8 now lifts his eyes to the lotus. He asks for puṣṭi, a life nourished and growing, and he places it in the hands of a glance. The thought to carry is that real growth is not only getting more; it is being fed in body, mind and heart, and being kind enough to feed others. This meditation is a devotional practice for attention and reflection; it does not promise a supernatural result or guarantee material wealth.
